Rijeka’s rail line is back after five days, Koper passes a million TEU, and the real risk this week is at sea

No. 02 · 21 October 2026 Croatia · Slovenia · EU · Black Sea · Western Balkans

The Zagreb–Rijeka line reopened to freight in the late afternoon of 15 October, five days after the grain train went into the ravine at Meja. That is shorter than the week first reported, but read the fine print: AdriaPorts says only freight is back, switches still have to be installed, and the signalling and safety equipment damaged in the accident still need repair. Treat this as a line running on a patch, not a repaired one. If you rerouted cargo through Koper or held it back last week, you can start releasing it, but I would keep a contingency booking in place until HŽ Infrastruktura confirms normal operation. Three incidents on the Rijeka approach in six weeks is a pattern, not bad luck.

Koper, meanwhile, reported one million TEU for the year to date on 14 October, ahead of schedule. The source gives no prior-year comparison or exact date, so I will not call it a record. What matters operationally is that the port is carrying that volume while its management is in Munich selling the southern route to Bavarian shippers. If you are moving Korea–Europe boxes toward southern Germany and Austria, Koper’s pitch is credible on transit time, but ask for the actual rail slot and dwell numbers rather than the headline.

The cost and reliability picture outside the Adriatic is what I would worry about. Xeneta’s September data, reported by AdriaPorts, puts global schedule reliability at 27%, the fourth monthly fall, and Far East–Europe at just 4% on-time with an average delay of 9.9 days, the worst since September 2021. At least 17% of scheduled Far East–Europe capacity is cancelled in October. Xeneta’s advice is to book early and ask which sailings are protected, and I agree. For a corridor business, a late vessel into Koper becomes a missed rail slot behind it. Separately, the EU leaders’ summit on 15–16 October asked the Commission to update its impact assessment of ETS2 without postponing it. ETS2 covers road transport fuel from 2028, so it will reach hauliers through diesel prices, not as a direct allowance bill. No decision yet, but price it into any multi-year road contract.

In the Black Sea, the news is bad for relief and reconstruction cargo. Euromaidan Press reports that between 13 and 15 October at least four merchant ships were hit by drones off Bulgaria and Romania, with the HS Dignity sunk and at least 17 seafarers reported missing. I have only one source for these details, so check before quoting. If it holds, insurers and charterers will reprice the Constanța and Danube options that last week’s Ukraine–Romania deal was meant to strengthen. In the same period an Egypt-led Arab-Balkans Corridor launched through Thessaloniki and Corridor X, naming Koper and Rijeka as rivals. It has no schedule or carrier on the Montenegrin Bar leg yet, so watch it rather than react to it.

Note on scope: six items passed verification this week, not ten. I left out anything I could not confirm by reading the source.

Xeneta의 9월 자료에 따르면 전 세계 선박 정시성은 27%, 극동–유럽은 4%에 평균 지연 9.9일이며, 10월 극동–유럽 선복의 최소 17%가 취소되어 조기 부킹과 운항 보장 여부 확인이 필수입니다. EU 정상회의는 ETS2 영향평가 재검토를 요청했으나 연기는 아니므로 2028년부터 도로 운송 연료비에 반영될 것을 계약에 감안해야 합니다. 흑해에서는 10월 13~15일 불가리아·루마니아 인근에서 상선 피격이 보도되어(단일 출처) 우크라이나 구호·재건 화물의 해상 경로 위험이 커졌습니다.

01
Zagreb–Rijeka reopens to freight

Freight traffic resumed in the late afternoon of 15 October after a five-day stoppage; passenger service is partial and repair work on switches and signalling continues.

Source: AdriaPorts, 16 October 2026

The port reported one million TEU since the start of the year, ahead of schedule; no prior-year comparison given. Management also presented the southern route to Bavarian partners in Munich.

Source: AdriaPorts, 14 October 2026

Xeneta data for September: Far East–Europe 4% on time, 9.9-day average delay, at least 17% of October Far East–Europe capacity cancelled.

EU asks for an ETS2 impact review

The 15–16 October summit asked the Commission to update its assessment; ETS2 (planned 2028) is not postponed and any change needs new legislation.

Source: AdriaPorts, 16 October 2026

Reported drone attacks on at least four ships off Bulgaria and Romania on 13–15 October, including a sinking with at least 17 seafarers missing; single source, treat with care.

Source: Euromaidan Press, 16 October 2026

Arab-Balkans Corridor launches

A private sea-rail-road route from Port Said and Alexandria via Thessaloniki and Corridor X to Niš, launched 29 September; a separate Bar (Montenegro) track has an MoU but no carrier or schedule.

Source: Enterprise, 8 October 2026
