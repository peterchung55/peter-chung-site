Ukraine’s export wagons wait 19 days at the western border, and its railway has lost more than 600 locomotives to the war

No. 04 · 4 November 2026 Ukraine · Bosnia and Herzegovina · Serbia · Montenegro · Kosovo

The number I would act on this week is 19 days. As of 23 October, according to Ukrzaliznytsia, 20,718 freight wagons were queued on the approaches to Ukraine’s western rail crossings, and the average wait to hand a wagon to the neighbouring railway was 19 days, or 32 days towards Batiovo on the Hungarian border. Only 837 of those wagons were at the border stations themselves; the rest are parked back across the network. Iryna Kosse of the Institute for Economic Research and Policy Consulting puts the delay on air-raid alerts that stop trains and suspend border checks, on a shortage of locomotives and crews in Zakarpattia, and on problems abroad, including a terminal at Constanța that stopped accepting trains. Turnaround to Constanța is now 12–13 days, against under ten before. Put that next to the Danube rates in No. 03 and neither of the alternative exits from Ukraine is cheap or quick at the moment. For relief consignments I now quote three weeks of border time on rail and say so in writing before the cargo moves.

Part of that queue is a traction problem, and this week the scale of it became clearer. The government says Russian attacks have damaged 605 locomotives. Ukrzaliznytsia plans to buy up to 60 second-hand units over the next six months, paid for partly from a $100 million World Bank grant, and Finland is donating ten Dv12 diesels with spare parts. A Polish operator, Laude Smart Intermodal, has now asked European companies to hand over their withdrawn diesels as well. Donated engines are not capacity until they are regauged, certified and crewed, so I am not counting on them before spring.

In the Balkans the news is slower and better. Bosnia and Herzegovina opened the Zenica Tunnel and the Nemila–Poprikuše section, another 21 km of the Corridor Vc motorway towards Ploče. In Belgrade, Serbia’s customs administration said its National Single Window for foreign trade is due to start on 1 January 2027. That is a date to put in every Serbian customer’s file now, because new single-window systems rarely start without a few weeks of rejected declarations.

Behind the logistics, Serbia’s count is complete. After re-runs at 53 polling stations on 1 November, the Student List has 53.01% against 38.98% for “Aleksandar Vučić – United Serbia”, on a turnout of 68.89%. The European Commission presents its enlargement package today. A draft seen by Radio Free Europe says Montenegro could finish accession talks in December and that Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and Kosovo have made no progress on the fundamentals. I will plan on the final text, not the leak, and report on it next week.

Note on scope: four logistics items and two context items passed verification this week. I found nothing new in the window from Luka Koper, Intereuropa or Pošta Slovenije. I left out Albania’s third-quarter port figures and a reported June 2027 signing date for Montenegro’s accession treaty, because I could not read either in a primary or trade source.

한국어 요약

이번 주 가장 중요한 숫자는 19일입니다. 우크라이나 철도공사에 따르면 10월 23일 현재 서부 국경 철도 접근 구간에 화차 20,718량이 대기 중이며, 인접국 철도로 인계하기까지 평균 19일, 헝가리 방면 바티보는 32일이 걸립니다. 원인은 공습경보로 인한 운행·통관 중단, 자카르파티아 지역의 기관차와 승무원 부족, 콘스탄차 터미널의 열차 접수 중단 등이며, 지난호에서 다룬 다뉴브 운임 급등과 함께 우크라이나의 대체 출구 어느 쪽도 싸거나 빠르지 않은 상황이어서, 구호 화물에는 국경 통과 기간을 3주로 잡아 사전에 서면으로 고지하고 있습니다. 정부 발표로는 공격으로 손상된 기관차가 605대에 이르러 우크라이나 철도는 6개월 안에 중고 기관차 최대 60대를 구매하고 핀란드는 Dv12 디젤 기관차 10대를 기증하지만, 궤간 변경과 인증을 거쳐 실제 운용되기까지는 시간이 걸릴 것입니다. 발칸에서는 보스니아 헤르체고비나가 5c 회랑 고속도로 21km(제니차 터널, 네밀라–포프리쿠셰 구간)를 개통했고, 세르비아 세관은 2027년 1월 1일 대외무역 국가 단일창구 가동을 예고했으며, 세르비아 총선 최종 집계는 학생 리스트 53.01% 대 38.98%로 마무리되었고 EU 집행위원회는 오늘 확대 패키지를 발표합니다.

Part A · On the ground

A1
Ukraine’s western rail crossings: 20,718 wagons, 19 days

Ukrzaliznytsia’s figures for 23 October show the longest queues towards Izov (5,820 wagons) and Chop (3,330), with a 32-day average wait towards Batiovo. Analysts blame air-raid stoppages, a shortage of locomotives and crews, and congestion on the Romanian side, where turnaround to Constanța has risen to 12–13 days.

Source: GMK Center, 27 October 2026
A2
Ukraine turns to second-hand and donated locomotives

With 605 locomotives damaged, Ukrzaliznytsia plans to buy up to 60 used units within six months, partly with a $100 million World Bank grant; Finland is giving ten Dv12 diesels and up to 15 more may come from other European partners. New Alstom locomotives are expected in the first quarter of 2027. Laude Smart Intermodal has since called on Europe to donate its withdrawn diesels.

Sources: RailFreight.com, 2 November 2026 · RailFreight.com, 3 November 2026
A3
Another 21 km of Corridor Vc opens near Zenica

Bosnia and Herzegovina opened the Zenica Tunnel and the Nemila–Poprikuše section. The EU put €61.5 million of grants into these sections and €870 million into Corridor Vc overall through the Western Balkans Investment Framework.

Source: The Region, 30 October 2026
A4
Serbia sets 1 January 2027 for its foreign-trade single window

At Cargo Conference Belgrade on 29 October, Serbian customs presented new automated procedures and the National Single Window launch date. Speakers put Serbia’s intermodal market at about 210,000 TEU in 2025 and said current terminals are already strained. This comes from the organiser’s press release.

Source: Ports Europe (press release), 2 November 2026

Part B · Behind the logistics

B1
Politics / elections
Serbia’s count is complete

Results from all 8,392 polling stations, published after re-runs at 53 stations on 1 November: Student List 53.01%, United Serbia 38.98%, turnout 4,444,310 voters or 68.89%. A follow-up to No. 03.

Logistics link: once the result is final, the new majority forms a government and starts appointing to transport, finance and customs.

Source: Government of Serbia, 2 November 2026
B2
EU integration
Enlargement package today; the draft favours Montenegro

Commissioner Marta Kos presents the package on 4 November. The draft seen by Radio Free Europe says Montenegro has met the closing benchmarks in all chapters and could end talks in December; Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and Kosovo made no progress on the fundamentals. By September, €193.3 million of the €1.6 billion Growth Plan grants had been paid out.

Logistics link: Bar and Montenegro’s borders move towards EU customs rules first; Growth Plan money for transport depends on the slower countries’ reforms.

Sources: European Western Balkans, 30 October 2026 · Srpske Novine, citing RFE, 30 October 2026

Country of the week · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Concrete on one side, conditions on the other. The 21 km opened near Zenica bring EU grants for Corridor Vc to €870 million through the Western Balkans Investment Framework. A further €522 million is reserved for such projects under the Growth Plan, but only once Bosnia and Herzegovina sets up the legal framework and carries out the required reforms. The Commission’s draft records no progress on the fundamentals and lists the country among those at high risk of “state capture”. Watch: whether the final package keeps that wording, and whether the €522 million is unlocked.

Sources: The Region, 30 October 2026 · Srpske Novine, 30 October 2026

People to know

Volodymyr Shemayev, Ukraine’s Deputy Minister for Restoration, Infrastructure and Transport, who set out Ukrzaliznytsia’s plan to buy second-hand locomotives and accept donated ones.

Tālis Linkaits, the new Director of the Transport Community’s Permanent Secretariat, who told the Belgrade conference that multimodal transport is the region’s biggest opportunity.

Sources: RailFreight.com, 2 November 2026 · Ports Europe (press release), 2 November 2026
