The Adriatic gateways keep adding quay; the rail behind them runs on a 2033 timetable

No. 01 · 14 October 2026 Croatia · Serbia · Ukraine

The figure I took from this week was not €677 million. It was 2033. That is roughly when Croatia expects to finish doubling Dugo Selo–Novska, the last single-track section of the RH1 corridor inside the country, under the contract HŽ Infrastruktura signed on 1 October. Works only begin in the first half of 2027 and are scheduled to run five years and ten months. Anyone routing rail freight between Zagreb and the Serbian border should treat those 83 km as a fixed constraint for the rest of this decade. I expect it to get tighter, not easier, once possessions for construction start, and the next stretch east, Okučani–Vinkovci, is still at the documentation stage.

At the other end of the same Croatian network, ICTSI’s Adriatic Gate terminal in Rijeka has passed four million TEU since 2011 and doubled its throughput in about five years. The cranes it added last year are sized for the largest ships now calling in the Adriatic. The hinterland it names is Austria, the Czech Republic, Hungary, Slovakia and Serbia, which is largely the same hinterland Koper serves. For a Korean shipper, two Northern Adriatic gateways competing hard for the same Central European boxes is good for rates and for leverage. But quay length is the easy part to build. What decides transit time is the rail out of the port, and for Serbia-bound cargo a Rijeka routing still crosses the single-track Novska section.

In Ukraine, the EBRD has agreed that Ukrainian Railways may use €130 million of a €180 million loan, originally meant for distributed power generation, for liquidity, critical repairs and keeping freight and passenger services running. Read plainly, this is money to keep the railway operating, not to expand it. The minister’s own figure is 550 locomotives damaged since the full-scale invasion began, more than 335 of them this year. For relief cargo, I plan the Ukrainian inland leg as the variable in the chain. The sea and EU rail legs are now the predictable part, so buffer days belong at the transfer point at the western border, not on the ocean leg.

What I am doing with this: for the European corridor we are structuring for KOICA’s Ukraine assistance, Koper stays the base routing and Rijeka is quoted as the alternate on every lot. In my cost models I am not assuming any improvement in Croatian east–west rail capacity before the early 2030s.

이번 주에 가장 중요하게 본 숫자는 6억 7,700만 유로가 아니라 2033년입니다. 크로아티아가 RH1 회랑에서 마지막 남은 단선 구간인 두고셀로–노브스카 83km의 복선화 계약을 맺었지만, 착공은 2027년 상반기이고 완공은 2032~33년경으로 예상되므로, 그때까지 자그레브–세르비아 국경 간 철도 운송은 이 구간을 고정된 병목으로 보고 계획해야 합니다. 리예카의 AGCT 터미널은 누적 400만 TEU를 넘기며 코페르와 같은 중부유럽 배후지를 두고 경쟁하고 있어 한국 화주에게는 운임 협상력이 생기지만, 실제 운송 시간을 좌우하는 것은 항만이 아니라 항만 뒤의 철도입니다. 우크라이나 철도공사는 EBRD 대출 1억 3,000만 유로를 유동성과 긴급 보수에 돌릴 만큼 운영 유지에 집중하고 있으므로, 구호 화물은 우크라이나 내륙 구간을 가장 불확실한 변수로 보고 서부 국경 환적 지점에 여유 일수를 두어야 합니다. 저희는 KOICA 우크라이나 지원 물자의 기본 경로로 코페르를 유지하고, 매 건 리예카를 대안 경로로 함께 견적하고 있습니다.

This week’s items

01
Croatia contracts the doubling of Dugo Selo–Novska on RH1

HŽ Infrastruktura signed a €677 million contract with Afcons Infrastructure on 1 October for a second track, ETCS Level 1 and new signalling over 83 km east of Zagreb. Construction is due to start in the first half of 2027 and last five years and ten months. The broadcaster HRT reports that EU funds cover 90% of the roughly €900 million total.

Source: RailFreight.com, 8 October 2026
02
Rijeka’s Adriatic Gate Container Terminal reaches four million TEU

The ICTSI-run terminal marked its four-millionth TEU since joining the group in 2011, having doubled from two million in about five years. The volume is backed by a €35 million programme completed in late 2025, which added two super post-Panamax ship-to-shore cranes and two hybrid RTGs. Inland markets served by rail and road include Austria, the Czech Republic, Hungary, Serbia and Slovakia.

Source: WorldCargo News, 2 October 2026
03
EBRD lets Ukrainian Railways redirect €130 million to liquidity and repairs

Ukrainian Railways and the EBRD amended an existing €180 million loan so that €130 million can go to operating liquidity, critical repairs and keeping cargo and passengers moving, instead of distributed power generation. Minister Mykola Kalashnyk announced it after meeting EBRD President Odile Renaud-Basso and said the ministry is aiming for access to the funds in the first weeks of October.

Source: Interfax-Ukraine, 7 October 2026
