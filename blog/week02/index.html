Rijeka’s line is back on a patch, and the real news this week is how much routing now depends on border workarounds, rail charges and sanctions

No. 02 · 21 October 2026 Croatia · Serbia · Ukraine · North Macedonia · Romania · Black Sea

On the ground, the Zagreb–Rijeka line took freight again on the afternoon of 15 October, five days after the derailment at Meja. Only freight is back, and switches and signalling still need repair, so I would keep a fallback booking until the operator says the work is finished. The more interesting trial this week is further east. LOG Rail and Željeznice Republike Srpske ran a test train between Croatia and Serbia on an alternative path that avoids the congested Tovarnik–Šid crossing, and LOG Rail’s chief executive says it saved a day even though the route is about 200 km longer. The report is paywalled after its lead, so I do not know the origin, destination or cargo, and one trial is not a service. But it tells me that a longer route that skips a border queue can beat the shorter one, which is the logic I use when planning Korea–Europe cargo toward Serbia.

Elsewhere the news is about rail that does not exist yet. Ukraine is preparing another standard-gauge line to Slovakia, with World Bank support for the preparatory work but no construction dates or amounts. North Macedonia has put its rail link to Bulgaria out to tender on the back of a €149 million EU grant signed in July, yet the article says a continuous connection is unlikely before the early 2030s because Bulgaria’s side is unfunded. Treat both as planning signals, not capacity. Closer to Koper, Trieste’s January–September numbers show 528,125 TEU, up 2.5%, with the third quarter alone up 22.3%, while its train count fell 14%. A competing Adriatic port growing on the water but losing trains is worth watching.

Behind the logistics, three decisions will shape cost and risk. Romania’s rail company is losing a 33% discount on track access charges for intermodal operators from December, after a legal review found the scheme was renewed every year since 2018 and applied unevenly; the article puts the total granted since 2018 at about €10 million but gives no figure for what operators will pay from December. Romania is the route Ukraine relief cargo uses to Constanța, so I would ask carriers how they price this in, though that link is my inference. In Serbia, the oil company NIS remains under US sanctions because of its Russian shareholders, and Romania has cleared the sale of its Romanian petrol-station arm to Argenta, a step separate from the Hungarian MOL talks. And net foreign investment into Serbia fell 2% to €1.26 billion in January–August. EU leaders also asked the Commission to refresh its ETS2 impact assessment without postponing it; ETS2 reaches hauliers through diesel prices from 2028.

The tail risk is at sea. One outlet reports at least four merchant ships hit by drones off Bulgaria and Romania on 13–15 October, with the HS Dignity sunk and at least 17 seafarers missing. I have only that source, so verify before quoting it. If it holds, insurers will reprice Black Sea options, and the Adriatic becomes relatively more attractive for relief cargo, even if rail capacity toward Ukraine is the harder constraint.

Note on scope: five logistics items and five context items passed verification this week. I left out anything I could not confirm by reading the source, and anything already covered in No. 01.

Part A · On the ground

A1
Zagreb–Rijeka reopens to freight

Freight resumed in the late afternoon of 15 October after a five-day stoppage; passenger service is partial and repairs to switches and signalling continue.

Source: AdriaPorts, 16 October 2026 · follow-up to No. 01
A2
Croatia–Serbia trial route avoids a congested border

A test train by LOG Rail and Željeznice Republike Srpske bypassed the Tovarnik–Šid crossing; transit time fell by a day on a route about 200 km longer. Origin, destination and cargo were not stated in the part I could read.

Source: RailFreight.com, 19 October 2026
A3
Ukraine plans another standard-gauge line to the EU

A new 1,435 mm line from Uzhhorod via Pavlovo to the Slovak border, plus modernisation of Korolevo–Khust; the work so far is preparatory and no funding amounts or construction dates are given.

Source: RailFreight.com, 19 October 2026
A4
North Macedonia tenders its rail link to Bulgaria

Backed by a €149 million EU grant signed in July 2026; Bulgaria’s side remains unfunded and a continuous connection is unlikely before the early 2030s.

Source: RailFreight.com, 16 October 2026
A5
Trieste’s third quarter jumps while its trains fall

January–September: 528,125 TEU (+2.5%), third quarter alone 165,502 TEU (+22.3%); rail traffic 5,206 trains, down 14.01%.

Source: Agenzia Nova, October 2026

Part B · Behind the logistics

B1
Law / regulation
Romania ends a 33% intermodal track-access discount

The discount, in place since 2018 and worth about €10 million in total, ends in December after the rail regulator’s legal objections; it is unclear whether it returns in another form.

Logistics link (my inference): Romanian rail charges matter for Constanța-bound Ukraine cargo.

Source: RailFreight.com, 21 October 2026
B2
Economy / sanctions
Romania clears sale of NIS’s petrol stations to Argenta

The buyer is a Romanian metalworking group moving into fuel; NIS itself remains under US sanctions tied to Russian ownership, with the MOL talks separate.

Logistics link: fuel and project-cargo supply in Serbia depends on the ownership outcome.

Sources: Gadget.ro, 15 October 2026 · Romania Insider
B3
Economy
Net FDI into Serbia slips 2%

€1.26 billion in January–August, per the central bank; the report does not say which countries or sectors led.

Logistics link: investment is where future cargo volumes come from.

Source: SeeNews, 19 October 2026
B4
Law / regulation
EU leaders ask for an ETS2 impact review

The 15–16 October summit asked the Commission to update its assessment; ETS2 (planned 2028) is not postponed.

Logistics link: road fuel cost, passed through to hauliers via diesel prices.

Source: AdriaPorts, 16 October 2026
B5
Security / geopolitics
Merchant ships reportedly hit in the western Black Sea

Drone attacks on at least four ships off Bulgaria and Romania on 13–15 October, including a sinking with at least 17 seafarers missing; single source, treat with care.

Logistics link: war-risk pricing on Constanța and Danube routing.

Source: Euromaidan Press, 16 October 2026

Country of the week · Serbia

Serbia is where this week’s themes meet. Its western expressway section from Slepčević to Badovinci on the Bosnian border (15.3 km) is due to be finished by 19 December, according to the acting infrastructure minister. Austria’s ÖBB plans to relaunch a direct Vienna–Belgrade passenger train in 2027. The government extended its fuel excise duty cut to 25 October. Watch whether the Croatia–Serbia alternative route becomes a regular service, and how the NIS ownership talks end.

Sources: SeeNews, 20 October 2026 · N1 · SeeNews, 19 October 2026
