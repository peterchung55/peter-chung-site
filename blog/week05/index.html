Drones are sinking ships bound for Romania and Bulgaria, and Koper has just had its busiest month

No. 05 · 11 November 2026 Ukraine · Slovenia · Serbia · Montenegro · Albania

For four weeks this briefing has covered Constanța as Ukraine’s other door. This week a ship heading for it was sunk. On 28 October the Contship Jet, a Greek-owned container vessel under the Liberian flag, sailing from Haifa to Constanța, was hit by three sea drones and went down in Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone with more than 400 containers on board. All 18 crew were rescued, helped by an hour’s warning from a French patrol aircraft. Reuters counts at least nine cargo ships hit off Romania and Bulgaria in October, with about 34 sailors dead or missing. Ambrey links nine of the ten ships it recorded to Ukrainian trade, and most were hit off Sulina while waiting to enter the Danube or Ukrainian waters. According to Lloyd’s List, some owners are now trying to refuse orders to Romanian and Bulgarian ports, and some masters do not want to go. For relief cargo routed through Constanța, it is now prudent to get the carrier’s acceptance of the voyage in writing before booking, and to price an Adriatic fallback alongside it.

Inland, Ukraine has started rationing. Rail deliveries of grain to the Greater Odesa ports fell to 10,300 tonnes in October, down 69.8% on September, while rail deliveries to the Danube ports rose 44.7%. Since 26 October Ukrzaliznytsia has imposed five restrictions on grain wagons to the border crossings, including a ban on grain via Batiovo, and from November it is going back to monthly plans and quotas for the western crossings. That is the answer to the 19-day queue in No. 04: fewer wagons get in, and the wait moves from the border to the shipper’s yard. Anyone who has not filed a November plan with the railway should not expect a slot.

On the Adriatic the numbers go the other way. Koper handled 117,163 TEU in October, a monthly record, beating the 114,397 it set in March. Intereuropa’s express road network has posted a notice that higher volumes may lengthen delivery times, and its fuel surcharge is 8.87% from 10 November. A busy Koper is good for the Korea–Europe corridor, but it also means booking further ahead.

Behind the logistics, the Commission’s enlargement package confirmed what last week’s draft said. Montenegro has closed 21 chapters and could close the rest by the end of the year; Commissioner Kos puts the earliest treaty signature in June 2027. Albania aims to finish talks in 2027. Serbia made no substantive progress, but its election result is now final, with 140 of 250 seats for the Student List, and the new assembly must meet within 30 days. A government in Belgrade by the new year is possible, and with it decisions on customs and rail.

Note on scope: four logistics items and two context items passed verification this week. Left out: a reported extension of the London market’s Black Sea war-risk listed areas and any premium figures, which could not be read in a primary or trade source; a Mediterranean surcharge notice from MSC, whose article could not be opened; and a reported 9% rise in Koper’s container volume with revenue above €100 million, which could not be found in any source.

Part A · On the ground

A1
A Constanța-bound container ship is sunk off Bulgaria

The Contship Jet was hit by three sea drones on 28 October and sank in Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone; a French Atlantique 2 patrol aircraft, now staying on in Romania for some weeks, gave the crew an hour’s warning. Ambrey recorded ten strikes on ships between 5 and 28 October, most off Sulina, and Lloyd’s List reports owners seeking to refuse orders to Romanian and Bulgarian ports. A follow-up to No. 02.

Sources: gCaptain (Reuters), 5 November 2026 · gCaptain, 5 November 2026 · Al Jazeera, 3 November 2026
A2
Ukraine rations grain wagons to its western border

Rail grain to the Greater Odesa ports fell to 10,300 tonnes in October, while the Danube ports took 205,300 tonnes by rail in the first 27 days of the month. Ukrzaliznytsia has imposed five restrictions since 26 October, including a ban on grain via Batiovo; 4,079 grain wagons were waiting for the crossings on 28 October. From November the railway returns to monthly planning and quotas. A follow-up to No. 04.

Sources: UkrAgroConsult (citing USM), 3 November 2026 · RailFreight.com, 28 October 2026
A3
Koper sets a monthly container record

The Port of Koper handled 117,163 TEU in October, above its previous best of 114,397 TEU in March. It passed one million TEU for the year on 9 October, the fifth year running. A follow-up to No. 02.

Source: AdriaPorts, 3 November 2026
A4
Intereuropa warns of longer delivery times

Intereuropa’s express road service says higher shipment volumes in its network may lengthen delivery and transit times. Its fuel surcharge is 8.87% from 10 November. No freely licensed photo of Intereuropa’s own facilities was found, so this item has none.

Source: Intereuropa, express service page (in Slovenian), seen 11 November 2026

Part B · Behind the logistics

B1
EU integration
Roadmaps for Montenegro and Albania; no progress for Serbia

The 4 November package gives indicative roadmaps to Montenegro, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine. Montenegro has provisionally closed 21 chapters and could close the rest by the end of the year, with the treaty signed in June 2027 at the earliest; Albania opened its last cluster and aims to conclude talks by 2027. Serbia made no substantive progress, and North Macedonia has yet to adopt its constitutional amendments. A follow-up to No. 04.

Logistics link: Bar and Durrës are the next ports likely to come under EU customs rules; the new gradual-integration dialogue covers transport.

Sources: European Western Balkans, 4 November 2026 · European Western Balkans, 4 November 2026
B2
Politics / elections
Serbia’s seats are allocated

The Republic Election Commission declared the final result on 5 November: 140 of 250 seats for the Student List, 103 for the Serbian Progressive Party and seven for ethnic-minority parties. The inaugural session of the assembly must follow within 30 days. A follow-up to No. 04.

Logistics link: the clock to a new government, and to new ministers for transport, finance and customs, is now running.

Source: Associated Press via NY1, 5 November 2026

Country of the week · Slovenia

A record month in Koper, a busy road network behind it, and a small rail idea to the south-east. At a meeting in Grosuplje attended by Monika Kirbiš Rojs, Minister for Cohesion and Regional Development, Slovenian and Croatian authorities, railway companies and the “Paths of the Future” grouping backed restoring the Novo Mesto–Karlovac rail link, which would complete a Ljubljana–Novo Mesto–Karlovac–Zagreb route. Re-opening is envisaged after 2030, and the border-region strategy for 2028–2034 also covers freight and access to multimodal transport. The partners want it named a strategic project in the next Interreg Slovenia–Croatia programme. Watch: whether it gets that status, and whether Koper’s October pace holds into the winter.

Sources: Croatia Week, 10 November 2026 · AdriaPorts, 3 November 2026
