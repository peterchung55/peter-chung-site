Koper’s second track has its rails and the H4 is open again, while Montenegro moves to close its transport chapter and Kosovo shuts Serbian firms out of its power market

No. 06 · 18 November 2026 Slovenia · Italy · Montenegro · Serbia · Kosovo · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Two pieces of Slovenian infrastructure moved this week, and both matter for anyone routing cargo through Koper. On the new Divača–Koper line the track works are finished and technical testing comes next; Slovenia is using the Rheda slab-track system for the first time. Koper’s own management named limited rail capacity as a constraint behind its record October (No. 05), so this is the line that matters most for Koper’s hinterland. But “rails laid” is not “trains running”, and the report gives no date for commissioning. Until a test and opening schedule is published, first-quarter rail plans via Koper should not assume the new line. On the road side the news is cleaner: DARS reopened the H4 in both directions on Monday 16 November after more than four months, so trucks over 3.5 tonnes coming from Italy can use Vrtojba and the H4 again instead of the Fernetti detour.

Koper is also leaning south. It now has nine weekly container services to Egyptian ports and two ro-ro services to Alexandria, and handled about 2.4 million tonnes of Egyptian imports last year. For a Korea–Europe corridor that relies on Suez, the Egyptian links are useful to know: they are relay options if a mainline call slips. In Trieste the story is about ownership and positioning rather than volume: a liquid-bulk terminal changed hands, and FS Logistix’s chief executive visited the port authority. No deal was announced, so the visit signals interest, not capacity.

Behind the logistics, the political calendar decides how fast the corridors move. Montenegro is expected to close Chapter 14 on transport, along with energy and regional policy, at an intergovernmental conference on 19 November. Transport is the chapter that brings its road and rail rules into line with the EU, which over time should make Bar and the Bar–Boljare route easier to plan around. Serbia’s new parliament met on 17 November and has 90 days to vote in a government, so decisions on Corridor X will wait on that clock. In Bosnia and Herzegovina a new three-member presidency took office on 16 November. Between Kosovo and Serbia, relations got worse: on 16 November Pristina banned electricity trading by Serbian companies and announced two months of emergency measures. This is about power, not goods, but it is another sign that the Kosovo–Serbia border is not a crossing to plan around without a fallback.

Further east, the Black Sea keeps getting more expensive. A WSJ analysis, reported by The Moscow Times, says more than 30 seafarers have been killed there this year and that ships entering via the Bosphorus are down 41% on October 2025. Relief or reconstruction cargo into Ukraine should still carry war-risk cost on any sea leg, with the overland routes across the EU’s land borders with Ukraine as the base case.

Update, 25 November: on 19 November Montenegro closed Chapters 16 (taxation) and 22 (regional policy) only; transport and energy stayed open after Croatia objected. See No. 07. This issue was published late, on 25 November, and appears here in date order.

Note on scope: five logistics items and five context items passed verification. No history or society item passed, and no verifiable logistics news turned up from Intereuropa, Pošta Slovenije, Albania or North Macedonia.

Part A · On the ground

A1
Divača–Koper: track works finished, testing next

The track works on the new Koper–Divača line are complete, and technical testing is the next stage. The line uses rigid slab track on the Rheda system, a first for Slovenia. No opening date is given.

Source: AdriaPorts, 14 November 2026
A2
The H4 reopens on 16 November

DARS said the H4 would reopen in both directions on Monday 16 November after more than four months of closures on the Rebrnice section, in force since 6 July. Trucks over 3.5 tonnes from Italy, diverted via Fernetti in the meantime, can again use Vrtojba and the H4. Works continue between Vipava, Ajdovščina and Vipavski Križ with one lane open each way.

Source: AdriaPorts, 13 November 2026
A3
Koper deepens its Egypt links

Slovenia–Egypt trade was €310 million in 2025 and €247 million in the first half of 2026, up 57%. In 2025 Koper handled about 2.4 million tonnes of imports from Egypt and 580,000 tonnes of exports. Nine weekly container services connect Koper with Alexandria, Damietta, Port Said and Abu Qir, plus two ro-ro services to Alexandria.

Source: AdriaPorts, 12 November 2026
A4
Trieste: a tank-farm sale and an FS Logistix visit

Alpha Trading bought 100% of Seastock Srl (130,000 cubic metres in 26 tanks, with rail and road access) from the Tosto Group, with about €20 million of investment planned over five to ten years. Separately, FS Logistix CEO Sabrina De Filippis met port authority president Marco Consalvo; the article describes no signed agreement.

Sources: AdriaPorts, 10 November 2026 · AdriaPorts, 9 November 2026
A5
The Black Sea is this year’s deadliest shipping lane

Citing a WSJ analysis, The Moscow Times reports that more than 30 seafarers have been killed in Black Sea attacks this year, that Ambrey has recorded more than 300 attacks, and that ships entering via the Bosphorus are down 41% on October 2025. Eight merchant ships were hit by drones in Romanian or Bulgarian waters in October.

Source: The Moscow Times, 12 November 2026

Part B · Behind the logistics

B1
Law and regulation
Montenegro set to close the transport chapter

An intergovernmental conference in Brussels on 19 November is expected to provisionally close Chapters 14 (transport policy), 15 (energy) and 22 (regional policy), after the 6 November closures brought Montenegro to 23 of 33. At the EU Enlargement Forum on 16 November, Commissioner Marta Kos said she is optimistic Montenegro can close all chapters by the end of the year. Update: only Chapters 16 and 22 closed on 19 November; see No. 07.

Logistics link: Chapter 14 covers road, rail and maritime rules, so closing it commits Montenegro’s carriers, its rail operator and the Port of Bar to EU standards on a known timetable.

Sources: EWB, 16 November 2026 · EWB, 16 November 2026
B2
Politics
Serbia’s new parliament convenes

The National Assembly met for the first time on 17 November; the Students’ List holds 140 of 250 seats and Vučić’s SNS 103, with the rest held by minority parties. A government must be voted in within 90 days, the caretaker SNS cabinet stays until then, and an early presidential election must be held by the end of the year.

Logistics link: customs reforms, rail procurement and EU-funded works on Corridor X will follow the new government’s calendar into early 2027 (inference, not reported).

Source: AP via KSAT, 17 November 2026
B3
Security and geopolitics
Kosovo bans Serbian companies from its electricity market

Prime Minister Albin Kurti announced a ban on buying, selling, importing, exporting or transiting electricity by companies registered in Serbia or owned by Serbian citizens, with an exception for Elektrosever under the Brussels energy agreement, and two months of emergency measures. He cited a December 2025 Energy Community ruling that Serbia must unblock the transmission lines.

Logistics link: every new Kosovo–Serbia measure raises the risk at their crossings, so routing into Kosovo via North Macedonia or Albania remains the safer default.

Source: N1, 16 November 2026
B4
Economy
Kosovo’s trade gap keeps widening

Kosovo customs data for January–October show imports above €6.3 billion, up 8.5%, and exports of €960 million, up 23.9%, leaving a deficit of €5.34 billion. China (€879.4 million), Germany and Turkey are the top suppliers, and Albania is the top export market (€182 million).

Logistics link: a market that imports almost seven times what it exports runs on full trucks in and empty trucks out, which is what sets the price of a backhaul from Pristina.

Source: The Region, 10 November 2026
B5
Politics
Bosnia and Herzegovina’s new presidency takes office

Željka Cvijanović, Denis Bećirović and Darijana Filipović were sworn in for four-year terms on 16 November, with Cvijanović as chair; Filipović succeeds Željko Komšić. In Republika Srpska, Nenad Stevandić was re-elected speaker and Savo Minić became president.

Logistics link: the Commission has called for a chief negotiator and a Growth Plan coordinator to be appointed urgently, and Corridor Vc funding depends on that, so the new presidency’s first appointments are the thing to watch (inference, not reported).

Source: EWB, 16 November 2026

Country of the week · Kosovo

Kosovo applied for EU membership in December 2022, and its Stabilisation and Association Agreement has now been in force for ten years. This year’s Commission report credits it with some progress toward a functioning market economy and says trade openness has increased significantly over the last six years, but finds only limited progress on transport infrastructure and renewable energy. The Reform and Growth Facility agreements were ratified in February 2026, but Donika Emini of BiEPAG estimates that Kosovo lost €43.8 million by missing the June reform deadline and that more than €100 million could be at risk if year-end deadlines are missed. She also notes that there has been no high-level Kosovo–Serbia meeting since 2023. For a logistics operator, Kosovo is an import market with a narrow export base, reached most reliably through North Macedonia and Albania. Watch: whether Pristina meets the year-end Growth Plan deadlines, and whether the two-month electricity emergency spills over into goods traffic.

Sources: EWB, 5 November 2026 · EWB, 9 November 2026 · JournalArta, 9 November 2026

People to know

Marco Consalvo, president of the Port System Authority of the Eastern Adriatic Sea (Trieste and Monfalcone). His authority is the counterpart in the DFDS relocation dispute that goes back to court on 2 December, and this month he hosted FS Logistix’s chief executive.

Source: AdriaPorts, 9 November 2026
