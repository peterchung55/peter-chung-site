Rijeka’s rail line is back, Koper passes a million TEU, and the decisions that will move cargo next year are being made in Brussels and Belgrade

No. 02 · 21 October 2026 Croatia · Slovenia · Western Balkans · Ukraine · EU · Black Sea

On the ground, the week was a story of one line reopening on a patch. The Zagreb–Rijeka line took freight again on the afternoon of 15 October, five days after the derailment at Meja, but switches and signalling still need repair, so I would keep a fallback booking until the operator says the work is finished. Koper reported one million TEU for the year to date, ahead of schedule. I will not call it a record because the source gives no prior-year figure, but the port is plainly carrying volume while it sells the southern route to Bavarian shippers. The weak point is outside the Adriatic: September schedule reliability was 27% worldwide and 4% on Far East–Europe, with 17% or more of October Far East–Europe capacity cancelled. A late vessel at Koper costs a rail slot behind it.

Behind the logistics, the slow part of the region is moving. On 12 and 13 October the EU held accession conferences with Montenegro, Albania, Ukraine and Moldova. Montenegro now has 21 of its 33 chapters provisionally closed, and Ukraine and Moldova opened two negotiating clusters covering 17 chapters. The Commission’s enlargement package, with accession roadmaps for four of those countries and a proposal for gradual integration, has been pushed back a week to 4 November. Early signs point to transport and energy among the areas for deeper market participation. For a corridor business that matters more than any single tender: customs alignment, rail rules and funding eligibility follow accession, in that order of boring detail.

The risks are political. Serbia’s oil company NIS is still held by Russian shareholders under US sanctions, and Romania has cleared the sale of its Romanian petrol-station arm to Argenta, a step in a restructuring that is separate from the Hungarian MOL talks. Fuel and project cargo in Serbia depend on how that ends. Kosovo’s parliament passed a law that the EU’s enlargement commissioner called unacceptable, which touches the Hague court handling cases involving Kosovo. I found no customs or transit consequence yet, but it is a funding and relationship risk to watch. And in the Black Sea, one outlet reports at least four merchant ships hit off Bulgaria and Romania between 13 and 15 October, with at least 17 seafarers missing. I have only that one source, so verify before quoting it. If it holds, insurers will reprice the Constanța and Danube options.

The common thread is that routing is being decided by accession timetables, sanctions and war risk as much as by track and berth. On the cost side, EU leaders asked the Commission to update its ETS2 impact assessment without postponing it; ETS2 will reach hauliers through diesel prices from 2028. I would write that into any multi-year road contract now.

Note on scope: four logistics items and six context items passed verification this week. I left out anything I could not confirm by reading the source.

Part A · On the ground

A1
Zagreb–Rijeka reopens to freight

Freight resumed in the late afternoon of 15 October after a five-day stoppage; passenger service is partial and repairs to switches and signalling continue.

Source: AdriaPorts, 16 October 2026
A2
Koper reaches one million TEU

One million TEU since the start of the year, ahead of schedule; no prior-year comparison given. Management also presented the southern route to Bavarian partners in Munich.

Source: AdriaPorts, 14 October 2026
A3
Global schedule reliability falls to 27%

Xeneta’s September data: Far East–Europe 4% on time, 9.9-day average delay, at least 17% of October Far East–Europe capacity cancelled.

Source: AdriaPorts, 13 October 2026
A4
Arab-Balkans Corridor launches

A private sea-rail-road route from Port Said and Alexandria via Thessaloniki and Corridor X to Niš, launched 29 September; a separate Bar (Montenegro) track has an MoU but no carrier or schedule.

Source: Enterprise, 8 October 2026

Part B · Behind the logistics

B1
Politics / EU integration
Accession talks move on four fronts

On 12–13 October Montenegro closed three more chapters (21 of 33 now provisionally closed), Albania closed one (4 of 33), and Ukraine and Moldova opened clusters 2 and 3 (17 chapters).

Logistics link: customs, transport and funding rules converge as chapters close.

Source: Council of the EU, 13 October 2026
B2
Politics / EU integration
Enlargement package slips to 4 November

It is expected to contain roadmaps for Albania, Moldova, Montenegro and Ukraine and a proposal for gradual integration, with early signs pointing to energy, transport, digital and defence.

Logistics link: transport is a candidate area for deeper market access.

Source: The New Union Post, 14 October 2026
B3
Economy / sanctions
Romania clears sale of NIS’s petrol stations to Argenta

The buyer is a Romanian metalworking group moving into fuel; NIS itself remains under US sanctions tied to Russian ownership, with the MOL talks separate.

Logistics link: fuel and project-cargo supply in Serbia depends on the ownership outcome.

Sources: Gadget.ro, 15 October 2026 · Romania Insider
B4
Politics / law
EU criticises Kosovo’s law on the Hague court

Commissioner Marta Kos called the amendments to the law on the Kosovo Specialist Chambers unacceptable and said they break Kosovo’s commitments to the EU.

Logistics link: relationship and funding risk; no customs impact found yet.

Source: Kosovo Online, 12 October 2026
B5
Law / regulation
EU leaders ask for an ETS2 impact review

The 15–16 October summit asked the Commission to update its assessment; ETS2 (planned 2028) is not postponed.

Logistics link: road fuel cost, passed through to hauliers via diesel prices.

Source: AdriaPorts, 16 October 2026
B6
Security / geopolitics
Merchant ships reportedly hit in the western Black Sea

Drone attacks on at least four ships off Bulgaria and Romania on 13–15 October, including a sinking with at least 17 seafarers missing; single source, treat with care.

Logistics link: war-risk pricing on Constanța and Danube routing.

Source: Euromaidan Press, 16 October 2026

Country of the week · Kosovo

A snapshot from this week’s data: the IMF raised its 2026 growth forecast to 3.5%, 0.2 points above April, and annual inflation reached 7.8% in September. That is a growing economy with rising prices. Watch how the EU responds to the Hague-court law, because it shapes the political climate for connectivity funding and project cargo.

Sources: SeeNews, 13 October 2026 · Trading Economics

People to know

Marta Kos, EU Commissioner for enlargement, who commented on the Kosovo law and has confirmed the 4 November date for the enlargement package.
