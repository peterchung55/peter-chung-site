Ships are now hit for where they have been, and the Danube is too low to carry what they leave behind

No. 06 · 25 November 2026 Ukraine · Slovenia · Serbia · Albania

Two weeks ago I started asking carriers for written acceptance of any voyage to Constanța. This week the question changed. Ambrey found that five of the six merchant ships attacked off Romania and Bulgaria since 13 November had called at a Ukrainian port at some point in 2026, even when the voyage they were on had nothing to do with Ukraine. The Betul 8 sank off Bulgaria on 16 November; its last Ukrainian call was in July. The next day the LPG carrier Gas Husky was hit off Bulgaria on a voyage from Egypt to Romania. Ambrey thinks it is a realistic possibility that the targeting looks at the whole fleet of an owner or manager. For a forwarder that means the vetting is no longer about my cargo or my port. It is about the ship’s trading history, and its sisters’. I have added a question on that to my booking checklist.

Inland, the river is failing too. The Ukrainian Danube Shipping Company says its iron ore exports, almost two-thirds of its traffic, have stopped completely. Depths of 1.0 to 1.5 metres on the Middle Danube are below the 1.6 metres its pushers need, and a barge that should carry 1,250 tonnes now loads 850. UDP has moved its fleet to the short run down to Constanța. Rail is taking up some of the slack: grain wagons handed to the western neighbours averaged 379.7 a day in November, up 34.4 on October, with Hungary taking the most. That is better than the queue I wrote about in No. 04, but it is still a quota system, and the wagons still have to get a plan.

Behind the logistics, the National Bank of Ukraine’s baseline now assumes the Greater Odesa ports stay blockaded until mid-2027, and Kyiv says a war-risk insurance fund with a €3 billion target will be launched in January. In Belgrade the new assembly has a speaker, Aleksandar Čupić, a lecturer in transport studies, and the 90-day clock to a government is running.

On the Adriatic, Luka Koper’s nine-month results show the other side of the same coin: containers up 2%, revenue up 9%, but dry bulk 5% below plan because the railway network could not carry it, and the port warns of rail restrictions from modernisation works into 2027. What I am doing: quoting Koper with rail lead times checked week by week, keeping the Black Sea in my offers only with a named ship and its history, and keeping Durrës in view for southern Balkan cargo.

Note on scope: four logistics items and three context items passed verification this week. I left out a reported Russian strike on Izmail and on a ship bound for Pivdennyi, which no independent source has confirmed; a Ports Europe estimate that the blockade will cost Ukraine 4.1% of GDP in 2027, because only the headline was readable; and a RailMarket analysis and two Safety4Sea reports on the Black Sea that I could not open.

Part A · On the ground

A1
Black Sea strikes now follow a ship’s trading history

Ambrey says five of the six merchant ships attacked in Romanian and Bulgarian waters since 13 November had called at Ukrainian ports in 2026; four were hit by aerial drones near the Sulina anchorage and two in Bulgarian waters. The Betul 8 sank off Bulgaria on 16 November, four months after its last Ukrainian call, and the LPG carrier Gas Husky was struck the next day; both crews were rescued. Ambrey expects more attacks and suggests war-risk screening may need to cover whole fleets. A follow-up to No. 05.

Source: Splash247, 19 November 2026
A2
Low water stops Ukraine’s Danube ore barges

The Ukrainian Danube Shipping Company says iron ore exports, almost two-thirds of its cargo, have stopped because depths of 1.0–1.5 m on the main routes are below the 1.6 m its pushers need; the shoals at kilometres 344 and 676 are the worst. Average barge loads fell from 1,250 to 850 tonnes, and the fleet has been switched to the Lower Danube run to Constanța. Forecasters do not expect a steady rise in the river before the end of November.

Source: GMK Center (citing UDP), 24 November 2026
A3
Grain wagons to the western border pick up

Ukrzaliznytsia data show an average of 379.7 grain wagons a day handed over at the western crossings in November, 34.4 more than in October. Hungary led with 118 a day, ahead of Slovakia (98.8), Poland (86.4) and Romania (76.5). A follow-up to No. 05.

Source: Ukrainian Shipping Magazine (citing Ukrzaliznytsia), 20 November 2026
A4
Koper grows on containers but is held back by rail

Luka Koper handled 972,298 TEU in January–September (+2%) and 646,209 vehicles (−6%); general cargo fell 16%, and dry bulk was flat but 5% below plan because of reduced railway capacity. Revenue rose 9% to €308.8 million and net profit 17% to €72.9 million. For 2027 it plans 1.338 million TEU, but warns of high port utilisation and rail restrictions from modernisation works. The container terminal has also taken two new Kalmar reachstackers. A follow-up to No. 05.

Sources: Luka Koper, 19 November 2026 · WorldCargo News, 17 November 2026

Part B · Behind the logistics

B1
Economy
Ukraine’s central bank plans for a blockade until mid-2027

The National Bank of Ukraine’s October inflation report estimates that attacks on infrastructure and the port blockade in the second half of 2026 will cut real GDP growth by 1.9 percentage points and cost $6.6 billion in foreign trade. Its baseline assumes the Greater Odesa ports stay blockaded until mid-2027, hitting grain exporters hardest.

Logistics link: plan Ukrainian export and relief routes through the Danube, rail and the Adriatic for at least the first half of next year.

Source: GMK Center (citing the NBU), 9 November 2026
B2
Investment / insurance
A €3 billion war-risk insurance fund for Ukraine

Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi told the German-Ukrainian Economic Forum in Berlin that Ukraine and its partners plan a war-risk insurance fund with a target capital of €3 billion, to be launched at the Ukraine Recovery Conference in Tallinn in January 2027. The EBRD and Aon are separately expanding their war-risk facility from €110 million to €150 million.

Logistics link: cover for warehouses, terminals and rolling stock is what decides whether reconstruction logistics gets private money.

Source: GMK Center (citing Ukrinform), 24 November 2026
B3
Politics
Serbia’s assembly elects a speaker; the 90-day clock starts

The new National Assembly met on 17 November, and on 19 November elected Aleksandar Čupić of the Student List as speaker, by 140 votes to 97. As speaker he also holds the head of state’s powers for now and must set a date for an early presidential election. No candidate for prime minister has been named; a government must be elected within 90 days. A follow-up to No. 05.

Logistics link: decisions on customs, rail and the single window wait for ministers; I do not expect them before February.

Sources: dpa via Yahoo News, 19 November 2026 · Al Jazeera, 17 November 2026

Country of the week · Albania

Durrës, Albania’s largest port, has a new director general. Andrea Shllaku, previously deputy director general and head of operations at the port authority, replaces Liburn Aliu, whose resignation was announced on 9 November without a public explanation. The planned €400 million Porto Romano commercial port north of Durrës is in doubt: the Commission’s 2026 report on Albania says the tender was cancelled after the only remaining bidder did not submit a final financial offer in time. Traffic is holding: the ministry reports 897,725 tonnes of cargo through Durrës in January–October (+1.23%) and 90,678 trucks (+2.72%). Watch: whether Porto Romano is re-tendered, and on what terms.

Sources: The Region, 11 November 2026 · inAlbania (from RTSH), 18 November 2026

People to know

Aleksandar Čupić, speaker of Serbia’s National Assembly and acting holder of the head of state’s powers, a university lecturer in transport studies.
