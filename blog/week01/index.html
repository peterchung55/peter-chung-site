A derailment shows how thin the Rijeka lifeline is, while Brussels and Zagreb quietly set the rules and money for the next decade

No. 01 · 14 October 2026 Croatia · Slovenia · Western Balkans · Ukraine · EU

The story of the week on the ground is a grain train that lost its brakes and went into a ravine at Meja on Saturday 10 October. Nobody was hurt, but the Zagreb–Rijeka line is shut and the port has lost its heavy-axle rail route. Reports say clearing and repair could take about a week. The only alternative, via Ljubljana, Pivka and Šapjane, is longer and has an axle-load restriction that rules out the heaviest trains (RailFreight says 18 t, TrasportoEuropa says 20 t; either way below the 22.5 t of the main line). If you have bulk or rail-dependent cargo for Rijeka this week, assume delay and ask your carrier now what they can reroute. For Koper this is a small, temporary opening, not a windfall: the diverted trains would have to use the same Slovenian route through Pivka. Koper itself reported one million TEU for the year to date, ahead of schedule, though the source gives no prior-year figure, so I would not call it a record.

The good news is on the same corridor but arrives late. Croatia signed a €677 million contract on 1 October to double-track the 83 km Dugo Selo–Novska section of RH1, the last single-track stretch on that route inside Croatia. Works start in the first half of 2027 and run nearly six years, so this is a 2032–33 capacity story, not something to book against. In the meantime expect construction disruption on a line that is already fragile. Elsewhere, Xeneta’s September data puts global schedule reliability at 27% and Far East–Europe at 4% on time, with at least 17% of October Far East–Europe capacity cancelled. Late vessels become missed rail slots, so book early and ask which sailings are protected.

Behind the logistics, the rules are changing faster than the track. On 6 October the Commission proposed letting candidate countries join parts of the EU internal market before full membership, naming energy, transport, digital and defence as the strategic areas, with participation reversible if reforms stall. Then on 12 and 13 October the EU held accession conferences: Montenegro now has 21 of its 33 chapters provisionally closed, Albania has 4, and Ukraine and Moldova opened two further clusters after Ukraine passed a minority-education law that Hungary had made a condition. The next clusters, including “Green Agenda and Sustainable Connectivity”, are targeted for December. For a corridor operator, transport in the early-integration list and a connectivity cluster waiting to open are the items to watch, because customs and rail rules follow them.

The political risk is uneven. Bosnia and Herzegovina voted on 4 October, and the shape of the next government will decide how fast anything moves on its side of Corridor Vc; that link is my inference, not something the source says. Kosovo’s parliament passed a law that the EU’s enlargement commissioner called unacceptable, which touches the Hague court handling cases involving Kosovo. I found no customs consequence yet. In the south and east, North Macedonia started works on a €139 million Corridor VIII motorway section, and Ukraine and Romania agreed to keep six daily trains running to Constanța, backed by €130 million of EBRD emergency money for Ukrainian Railways. For relief and reconstruction cargo, Black Sea routing through Romania is getting institutional support, and the Adriatic is not yet part of that conversation.

Note on scope: six logistics items and four context items passed verification. I left out anything outside the window or that I could not confirm by reading the source.

Update, 21 October: freight traffic on the Zagreb–Rijeka line resumed on the afternoon of 15 October, with repairs to switches and signalling still under way. See No. 02.

Part A · On the ground

A1
Rijeka loses its rail link after a derailment

A loaded grain train derailed at Meja near Rijeka on 10 October, shutting the Zagreb–Rijeka line; clearing could take about a week and the Slovenian alternative is longer and axle-load limited.

Sources: RailFreight.com, 12 October 2026 · TrasportoEuropa, 12 October 2026 · AdriaPorts, 11 October 2026

Croatia signs the big RH1 double-track contract

€677 million for Dugo Selo–Novska (83 km), EU expected to cover about 90% of the project cost, construction from H1 2027 for nearly six years.

Source: RailFreight.com, 8 October 2026

Koper reaches one million TEU

One million TEU since the start of the year, ahead of schedule; no prior-year comparison given.

Source: AdriaPorts, 14 October 2026

Part B · Behind the logistics

B1
Politics / EU integration
Commission proposes gradual integration for candidates

The 6 October communication would let candidate countries take part in selected parts of the internal market early, with energy, transport, digital and defence as strategic areas; participation depends on reform progress and can be reversed, and the Council and Parliament may take up all, some or none of it.

Logistics link: transport is on the early-integration list, so rail and customs rules may open up before membership.

Source: The New Union Post, 6 October 2026
B2
Politics / EU integration
Accession talks advance on four fronts

On 12–13 October Montenegro closed three more chapters (21 of 33 provisionally closed) and Albania one (4 of 33); Ukraine and Moldova opened clusters 2 and 3 after Ukraine passed a minority-education law on 12 October that Hungary had required, with clusters 4 and 5 targeted by December.

Logistics link: cluster 4 covers “Green Agenda and Sustainable Connectivity”.

Sources: Council of the EU, 13 October 2026 · The New Union Post, 13 October 2026
B3
Politics
Bosnia and Herzegovina votes

General elections were held on 4 October; the EU’s joint statement frames the result as setting the country’s direction for the coming years and asks for authorities to be formed without delay.

Logistics link (my inference): Corridor Vc and Bosnian infrastructure decisions depend on the next government.

Source: Joint statement by HR/VP Kallas and Commissioner Kos, 6 October 2026
B4
Politics / law
EU criticises Kosovo’s law on the Hague court

Commissioner Marta Kos called the amendments to the law on the Kosovo Specialist Chambers unacceptable and said they break Kosovo’s commitments to the EU.

Logistics link: relationship and funding risk; no customs impact found.

Source: Kosovo Online, 12 October 2026

Country of the week · Montenegro

Montenegro is the front-runner on paper: on 12 October it closed three more chapters, leaving 21 of 33 provisionally closed. On the ground, Belgrade–Bar rail freight fell to 233,000 tonnes in the first quarter of 2026 from 308,000 a year earlier, and the EU is putting €175.6 million (an EIB loan plus a Commission grant) toward a €230.8 million upgrade of the Bar–Golubovci section. In late July the Alexandria and Montenegrin port authorities signed an MoU, but there is no named carrier or sailing schedule yet. Watch whether the Bar track gets a carrier and a schedule.

Sources: Council of the EU, 13 October 2026 · Enterprise, 8 October 2026
