# Weekly Briefing — standing brief

Published every Wednesday at peterchung.kr/blog/weekNN/. When the owner asks for "this week's briefing",
follow this brief, then publish it as described in CLAUDE.md ("Adding a Weekly Briefing issue").

Draft this week's issue of Peter Chung's "Weekly Briefing" for his website peterchung.kr

WHO THIS IS FOR
Hyung-Eun (Peter) Chung is a Korean maritime and multimodal logistics executive, 40+ years: ship's deck officer, shipping agency, KOICA's sole and exclusive shipping agent 1996-2005, project forwarding for the oil and energy sector, now chairman of the ISAAK Humanitarian Relief Logistics Task Force and rebuilding Korea Freight Services around a Korea-Europe corridor through the Port of Koper. The briefing is written in his first-person voice: a practitioner's read, dry, specific, no press-release language, no hype.

SCOPE
Slovenia plus the nine countries where the Intereuropa Group has subsidiaries: Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, Kosovo, North Macedonia, Albania, Ukraine. Subjects that matter: the Port of Koper and other Adriatic ports, rail corridors (RH1/Corridor X, Corridor Vc, Adriatic-Ionian), road and border-crossing capacity, customs and transit (T1), intermodal terminals, Intereuropa and Pošta Slovenije themselves, EU connectivity funding, Ukraine reconstruction logistics and relief corridors.

WHAT TO PRODUCE
1. Search the web for news published in the LAST 7-10 DAYS across that scope. Use several searches. Prefer trade press (RailFreight, Container News, WorldCargo News, Port Technology, SeeNews, Slovenia Times, AdriaPorts, Intereuropa and Luka Koper's own newsrooms) and primary sources over aggregators.
2. Pick 3 to 6 items that a logistics professional would actually act on. Quality over count — three solid items beat six thin ones. Never pad.
3. VERIFY each item by fetching the article itself before you write about it. Use only figures, names and dates you have actually read. Never invent a number, a quote or a date. If you cannot verify something, leave it out.
4. Write one headline-style title (a sentence, not a label) and a 3-4 paragraph first-person "take" in English that connects the week's items to what they mean operationally — routing, capacity, timing, cost, risk. Then a condensed Korean version of the same take, 3-5 sentences, as a single paragraph.
5. NEVER reproduce article text. Write original one-to-three sentence summaries in your own words and always link the source.
6. PHOTOS — one lead photo for the issue plus one photo per item, chosen to match the content.
   a. First choice: the photo the source article uses. Open the article, find its main image and its
      credit line, then check the rights holder's terms (newsroom, press-kit or terms-of-use page).
      Use it only if the holder explicitly allows reuse on a site like this (an open licence, or press
      images released for media use). The credit line alone ("© X") is NOT permission. If the terms
      forbid reproduction or say nothing, do not use it. Never hotlink it either.
   b. Otherwise: a freely licensed photo from Wikimedia Commons (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public
      domain) of the exact place, line, terminal or operator in the story. Look at it before choosing.
   c. Save to images/blog/weekNN/ (lead about 1600px wide, item photos about 960px), and give every
      photo a caption with author, a link to the file page, and the licence with a link.
   d. Tell the owner which source photos were checked and why each was or was not used.
